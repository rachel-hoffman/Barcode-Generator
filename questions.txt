(1)
Both the filter1 and filter2 filters use nested for loops to iterate through all pixels. However, filter 1 has 'j < width' in the outer loop and 'i < height' in the inner loop, which means it is going through the image column-by-column. In contrast, filter 2 swaps these two loops, and subsequently works its way through the image one row at a time. Since we know that the indexing in the image follows row-major order, filter 2 should have better performance because its cache use will be much more efficient. Filter 2 makes use of spatial locality by following the index flow of the image, so that we will have high chances of a cache hit.

(2)
The eval.sh script ran each program with a variety of image sizes, conducting each test 10 times and placing those 10 tests each in their own file in the data directory. Looking at the raw data, there is definitely some variance in the time taken for any given test. That said, it is clear to me that filter 2 has lower latency overall. For example, I compared filter1.2048.out and filter2.2048.out, which are filters 1 and 2 taking on our largest test data. Of all 10 tests, filter 1 never took less than 0.1 seconds, and even got up to over 0.2 seconds in one trial. Comparing that to filter 2, I noted that all of the tests stayed under 0.05 seconds, with most only taking 0.02 seconds. So, although we see some variance (I would estimate at most ~0.1 for filter 1 and ~0.02 for filter 2) it is still clear looking at the data that filter 2 is repeatedly and significantly quicker than filter 1. There may be some exceptions to this in the smaller data sizes, however, the 2048 tests exaggerate and highlight the issues for us.

(3)
Looking at the cachegrind outputs for both filters 1 and 2, the first thing I notice is that the D1 misses are vastly different. The two have about the same number of references (D refs) are about the same (60,434,384 and 60,434,379), however the D1 misses I have are 1,346,238 for filter 1, and only 394,684 for filter 2. That is about a million more misses on filter 1 than filter 2. Based on what we have learned this week, I would attribute the difference in timing behavior to the fact that filter 2 takes into account spatial locality and row-major order, and filter 1 does not. This is going to limit cache misses, whereas filter 1 will not have this same efficiency. This matches what I see in the testing as well.
